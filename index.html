Eco-Friendly Air Tips

Simple habits that make a big difference every day.

🌿

Open Windows Daily

Even 10 minutes of fresh air circulation in the morning can flush out overnight VOCs and CO₂ build-up.

🌿

Ditch Synthetic Candles

Paraffin candles release toluene and benzene. Swap to beeswax or soy candles with cotton wicks for cleaner air.

🌿

Vacuum with HEPA Filters

Standard vacuums recirculate fine particles. HEPA-filtered vacuums trap 99.97% of dust, pollen, and pet dander.

🌿

One Plant per 100 sq ft

NASA research suggests placing one medium plant per 100 square feet for measurable air purification effects.

🌿

Choose Low-VOC Products

Paints, cleaners, and air fresheners can off-gas harmful chemicals. Look for "Low-VOC" or "Zero-VOC" labels.

Key Indoor Pollutants

Understanding what's in your air is the first step to improving it.

PM2.5
Particulate Matter

Fine particles under 2.5 microns from cooking, candles, and smoke. Small enough to penetrate deep into lung tissue and enter the bloodstream.

Sources: cooking, candles, smoking, dust
Risk Level: Very High
VOCs
Volatile Organic Compounds

Chemical gases from paints, cleaners, and new furniture. Includes benzene and formaldehyde — both classified as carcinogens by the WHO.

Sources: paint, furniture, cleaning products
Risk Level: High
CO₂
Carbon Dioxide

Produced by breathing, accumulates in sealed rooms. Elevated CO₂ causes drowsiness, poor concentration, and reduced cognitive performance.

Sources: breathing, gas stoves, poor ventilation
Risk Level: Moderate
CO
Carbon Monoxide

Colourless, odourless gas from incomplete combustion. Prevents blood from carrying oxygen — even small amounts can be life-threatening.

Sources: gas heaters, stoves, car exhaust
Risk Level: Critical
Mould Spores
Biological Contaminant

Microscopic fungal spores that thrive in damp, poorly ventilated areas. Trigger allergic reactions, asthma attacks, and respiratory infections.

Sources: damp walls, bathrooms, leaks
Risk Level: High
Pet Dander
Biological Allergen

Microscopic flakes of skin shed by pets. Can remain airborne for hours and settle into carpets, bedding, and upholstery for months.

Sources: cats, dogs, birds, rodents
Risk Level: Moderate

WHO Air Quality Guidelines (2021)

The World Health Organization recommends PM2.5 annual mean of 5 µg/m³ and 24-hour mean of 15 µg/m³. Most cities worldwide exceed these limits — making indoor air quality management critical for everyone.
